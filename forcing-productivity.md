## Forcing Productivity

1.  Aggressively minimize the time between **deciding** to do something and the time you **start** doing it. Got this idea from Alex Hormozi.

2. Ruthless prioritization - always focus on the highest leverage activities. Everything else is noise.

> "You will beat 90% of your competition if you force yourself to (**consistently**) do just these first two."

3. Nix distractions - distractions are the death knell of productivity. Losing focus while working on [insert high leverage activity] is bad enough. Now marry that with the time loss of having to context switch back into focus mode. It takes considerable mental energy to refocus yourself on a task. It's best to avoid distractions altogether. 
    - Turn all devices on DND
    - Nix the lights. Try working in a dark enclosure with nothing but your laptop.
    - Block yourself from accessing certain apps (social media) on your phone and laptop.
    - Limit the number of tabs you can open (there are extensions for this)
    - Commit to silence / no distracting videos or background music. Maybe only ambient sounds, if you must - wilderness ambience, airplane terminal ambience.

4. Grok your incentive structures...

5. Aggressive deadlines - setting impossible deadlines for yourself can help you pull months and years forward. You'll probably end up delivering later than your (very aggresive) expectations, but it may still be faster than every one of your competitors. The Tesla roadster (which was first debuted in 2006) had production delayed until 2008. And it was ***still*** the first battery-powered car. Aggressive deadlines also changes your mindset from “this is impossible” to “maybe it’s not _that_ impossible”.

6. Batch tasks - find ways to batch similar tasks together. 
- You can eat and do laundry at the same time.
- Workout and check your email or learn new words. 

7. Find the correct motivation(s)
    - Ask “what is the very next step I need to take?”. Ambiguous goals are MUCH less exciting to tackle than ones that are clear & well defined.
    - Pithy motivational sayings / quotes.
    - Motivational music - you have a YouTube playlist for this. These are good for hyping yourself up enough to start a task.
    - Viewing / remembering acts of heroism, bravery, athleticism.
    - Ask AI to motivate you
    - Get a rival. 
        - Zack motivated you to be a better trumpet player and you got 1st chair Wind Ensemble, 1st Chair Region Band, sat All State & All State Jazz.
        - Paul challenged you to be a better software engineer.
        - If no rivals, think about anyone who has ever doubted your ability to succeed. Give yourself something to prove.
    - Most importantly believe in your mission, implicitly. Think about the excitement you get when thinking about delivering Velina to market - the opportunity to be one of the first to tap this market, the different ways to monetize in the future, how anxious you get while blocked because something else is in the way. This feeling doesn’t last forever, but it’s a good signal of your passion. 
    - Do you have any dependents that would be negatively affected if you fail? Not at the moment, but think of your future descendants. Saul’s decision to take the easy path (to spare Agag) caused heartache for Israel generations later. Also, Israel’s decision to not drive out the inhabitants of Canaan caused them to be in the trouble they’re in today.
    - Think about hard problems you’ve overcome already.

    - “Look 100 years ahead”
		Try to prioritize things (or at least think about them) in terms of “years of impact”
		Higher impact things like witnessing, preaching the gospel, living faithfully, will have “100 year impact”
        - Think of the way different Bible characters lived and the impact they have on us even today.


8. Asynchronous communication - self-explanatory

9. First principles - this idea of working from first principles. Take some set of absolute truths that are not derived from other truths to then reason your way through a problem to form a (probably novel) solution. It’s a fascinating thing. One might see it as no more than “using common sense”, but first-principles thinking, I think, implies a bit more intentionality.
