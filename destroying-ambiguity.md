## Framework for Destroying Ambiguity

### Navigating information deficiency

"I don’t know..."
- "...but I know someone who might."
- "...yet, but I will soon."
- "...but [if I had to guess / my gut is telling me] we could try X, Y, or Z."
- "...and I’ll defer this decision to your best judgement."
- "...and I don’t know to move forward." (This is fine. You don't have to know everything!)

---

### “Do or do not?” - Risk v. Yield v. Reversibility

"risk" ~ _the **level of potential loss or injury** associated with some uncertain, non-guaranteed scenario_

"yield" ~ _the **level of reward** gained from succeeding_

"reversibility" ~ _the degree to which a decision (or the effects thereof) can be undone_

Low risk & low yield? Don’t bother...

High risk & low yield? DON'T!

Low risk & high yield? DO IT!

High risk & high yield? Carefully consider the risks. What's the worst case scenario? It's not pretty, but is it a **tolerable** risk for you and your family? If so, bias TOWARDS DOING!

Reversible? Bias towards doing

Irreversible? Think VERY carefully

### The Two Sides of Every Problems
- The Solution
- The Execution

Just coming up with a solution is not sufficient. You have to execute it as well.

---

### Knowing When to Delegate
***Can*** do - ***Should*** do - ***Must*** do

Do the things that ***you*** must do. 

Things you ***can*** do can be delegated to others (theoretically, anyone else _can_ do these things as well). 

Things you probably ***should*** be the one doing can be delegated on a case-by-case basis.
